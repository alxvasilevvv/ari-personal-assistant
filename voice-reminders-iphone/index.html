The problem with notification banners

iPhone reminders — from Reminders, Calendar or third‑party to‑do apps — arrive as silent banners. They stack up, get swiped away, or land in a notification pile that you only open when you're already behind schedule. Voice capture via Siri works, but the delivery has always been the weak point: the reminder is stored, not heard.

How Ari delivers a voice reminder

1 · Say it, hands‑free

Call Ari with one tap, from Siri ("Call Ari"), or from the Lock Screen. Just speak: “remind me to submit the report Friday at 4”, “call me at 9 about the meeting”, “wake me at 7 with an alarm”.

2 · It lands where you expect

Ari creates entries in Reminders and events in Calendar. Alarms and timers ring like the system ones and show a countdown in the Dynamic Island.

3 · She calls you

At 9:00 your phone rings like an ordinary call — that's Ari, and she tells you why she's calling. Morning briefings, reminders and important emails arrive on your schedule instead of in a silent stack.

Quick setup

  1. Install Ari (free) and give her a name — “Call Alex” works if you like.
  2. Tap the “Call Ari” button (app, widget, Lock Screen, Control Center or the Action button) and say what you need.
  3. At the scheduled time, answer the call. Irreversible actions (messages, calls to other people) always ask for your confirmation first.

Scenarios people use

  • “Remind me to take the medicine in 30 minutes” → rings at the right time.
  • Driving: “call me when I need to pull over for a break” — no tapping, no looking at a phone.
  • Shift work: “call me at 5:40 to pick up my kid from school”.
  • Morning: a briefing with today's calendar and the emails that matter.
